## How a note earns its place

A field note should help the next person move faster or see more clearly. Keep it short enough to use, specific enough to trust, and linked to its source.

### A useful note usually has

- **A question:** what were we trying to find out?
- **A finding:** what did we learn, in plain language?
- **A trail:** where can someone check the source or continue exploring?
- **A date:** when was this true or verified?

## A small map for uncertain things

When a source leaves room for interpretation, write down three separate layers. It keeps a promising idea from quietly turning into a fact.

### 1. Observation

Record what the source directly shows or says. Include the date, the relevant passage or measurement, and a link someone else can open. Keep this part as close to the source as you can.

### 2. Interpretation

Describe what you think the observation might mean, and why. Mark it as an interpretation. If more than one explanation fits, keep the alternatives visible instead of choosing the most interesting one too early.

### 3. Open question

Name what you still do not know. A useful question points toward evidence that could clarify the picture: another source, a repeat measurement, a missing date, or a different point of view.

### Leave a trail someone can follow

A compact note can use this shape:

> **Question:** What are we trying to understand?  
> **Observation:** What does the source directly tell us?  
> **Interpretation:** What might that mean, and what else could explain it?  
> **Open question:** What evidence would help us decide?  
> **Source and date:** Where did this come from, and when was it checked?

The aim is not to remove uncertainty. It is to mark its edges, so the next reader can see where the evidence ends and the mapmaker begins.
