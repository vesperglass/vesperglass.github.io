# Vesperglass Encyclopedia: content ideas and backlog

Working tracker for new reference forms and encyclopedia additions. Keep it in step with the site and update it during each scheduled encyclopedia-growth run.

## Editorial guardrails

- Treat the atlas and currently written setting as the source for established names and facts.
- Label reconstructions, proposed details, and invented examples as **interpretation**; do not silently promote them to canon.
- Separate setting lore from implemented simulation behavior. Expeditions, gathering, refining, and persistent operations remain planned unless the site itself changes.
- Keep folios readable as reference pages: clear subject, concise context, useful sections, image descriptions, and links to related entries.
- Prefer additions that connect places, materials, vessels, and journeys so the setting feels inhabited without making unsupported claims.

## Candidate content types

| Content type | What it can add | Editorial treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specimen cabinet cards | Distinct materials, observed traits, handling notes, and relationships to craft | Mark invented classifications and samples as interpretive until established elsewhere |
| Annotated keeper log pages | A dated observation or maintenance note that reveals routine and voice | Label as an imagined document; do not imply a real expedition system |
| Vessel and instrument dossiers | Parts, purpose, operating principles, and repair vocabulary for trains or observatory tools | Distinguish visible design from inferred function |
| Route and crossing records | Named Gate journeys, departure and arrival descriptions, and route terminology | Frame as setting history or interpretation; do not claim a live route simulation |
| Place customs and daily practices | How keepers use shared spaces, prepare for departures, or preserve records | Mark customs as proposals until adopted as established lore; present them as optional social practices, not required simulation steps |
| Stellar body field plates | A survey of a kind of remote world, its landscape, and possible materials | Use as illustrative worldbuilding, not as a claim about generated Galaxy data |
| Oral histories and remembered sayings | Conflicting recollections, local phrases, and knowledge passed between keepers | Attribute each voice to an imagined source and retain uncertainty |
| Glossary crosswalks | Linked terms that compare material, vessel, place, and travel language, including distinctions between a named place and a structure or role | Identify the source for each term and state what it does not establish; don't let shared nouns imply a route or common construction |
| Archive catalog cards | A single chart, instrument note, or record with provenance and a short annotation | Identify each card as a proposal unless the site establishes the record as canon |
| Materials handling labels | Small notes about how objects are packed, stored, or moved between places | Mark handling methods as interpretive unless directly established in a folio |
| World relationship indexes | A compact cross-reference showing established links among places, materials, vessels, and journeys | Show only documented relationships; do not imply a physical route or system where none is established |
| Keeper phrasebook slips | Greetings with brief context and optional replies, making everyday interactions legible | Mark wording as proposed when no account records it; present use and replies as choices, not binding custom |
| Seasonal light calendars | A compact record of how keepers might name recurring changes in daylight or starlight | Present as an imagined cultural reference, not a real-time cycle or simulation mechanic |
| Instrument care notes | Brief annotations about how an imagined keeper might protect or inspect a reconstructed instrument | Label the equipment and routine as interpretation; do not imply an implemented maintenance system |
| Catalog status ribbons | Compact provenance labels that distinguish a named place, a proposed record, an unnamed subject, and absent date or coordinate information | Keep the ribbon as editorial apparatus; never match an unlocated account to a point shown on the separate Galaxy map |
| Plan reading keys | A small legend that names features visible in an architectural or garden plan and separates depiction from established description | Mark depicted components, cautious readings, and unrecorded details separately; do not infer scale, orientation, planting, or use without an account |
| Relationship evidence tables | A cross-folio index that groups documented connections by lore, viewer navigation, or visual presentation | Cite the relevant folios and state the limit of each connection; do not turn editorial links into routes |
| Comparative observation strips | A paired record that identifies agreement, difference, and the question left open between two readings | Mark proposed leaves and absent dates, causes, or provenance; don't promote a comparison into a finding |
| Room sound notes | Descriptive accounts of how a reconstructed room might carry voices, footsteps, or silence | Tie each cue to a pictured position and possible conversational effect; keep every sound proposed and do not imply an audio recording |
| Material state comparison cards | A paired reading of how an established substance is described at rest and in motion | Stay within the source account's sensory details; do not infer quantity, vessel design, or handling procedure |
| Process-to-plate keys | A sequence that maps named account steps to the relevant reconstructed tools in an illustrated folio, with an explicit “not shown” state when the plate offers no counterpart | Separate recorded actions from visual matches; identify which objects are pictured and which are named in accounts; leave settings and undocumented operations open |
| Instrument plate inventories | A structured visual key for a place plate that pairs each pictured instrument with a cautious reading and unanswered provenance or specification questions | Treat names as descriptive labels; keep depicted arrangement separate from an established collection, procedure, or implemented tool |
| Reader pronunciation keys | A practical guide with syllable breaks and stress cues for unfamiliar place, vessel, or material names | Label readings as dialect-sensitive English aids when no account preserves local speech; do not assert a canonical pronunciation |
| Illustrated flora notes | A caption-key distinguishing plants depicted in a place plate from catalogued species or established botanical facts | Describe the image without assigning biology, cultivation, or a keeper's practice unless an account supports it |
| Evidence-to-field matrices | A compact table for each proposed artifact that maps every catalog field to its supporting account and names the remaining unknowns | Treat the matrix as editorial apparatus, not an in-world form; keep unsupported fields blank and link the source folio |
| Journey docket cards | A compact departure-to-arrival record that separates a crossing's described sequence from absent route metadata | Label the docket as proposed unless a dated crossing record exists; leave passenger, destination, schedule, and duration unknown rather than implying a travel log or live route system |
| Postal issue plates | An illustrated first stamp issue and related seal assembled from named Vesperglass architecture | Label the service and designs as proposals until an account establishes a postal institution; do not imply routes, rates, or delivery behavior |
| Cancellation-mark registers | A small key to proposed postmarks that distinguishes a design motif from the date, place, or route of an actual dispatch | Keep each mark interpretive unless a stamped letter or postal account supplies provenance; do not infer a route from a Gate or place image |
| Mailpiece face layouts | A labeled front-of-envelope study showing how a proposed stamp and address fields could share a page | Mark the layout as an editorial schematic; leave names, addresses, destinations, routes, and delivery practices unestablished |
| Berth-state comparison cards | A two-state reading of a vessel's approach and alongside position relative to a platform | Separate described berth relationships from the atlas's path or animation; leave spacing, orientation, track, and docking procedure unrecorded |
| Evidence-lineage strips | A compact path from a visual motif to its named source folio and the inference made for a new artifact | Cite the named source and label the motif as a proposal; state the limit of the relationship in the same row |
| Threshold and aperture profiles | A source-aware comparison of a pictured threshold's stable form and the event described around it | Keep image evidence, account language, and unanswered architectural questions distinct; do not infer moving parts or a crossing mechanism |
| Repository-to-observation boundaries | A comparison of a named record-keeping place with a nearby observation account that states whether any specific record is shared | Keep thematic relevance separate from common authorship, date, instrument, catalog ownership, or matching map subject |
| Cross-record corroboration notes | A source-horizon table showing which stage or claim each neighboring account supports and which link between records remains unproven | Compare scope and provenance, not just matching vocabulary; do not merge undated accounts into one event |
| Image-to-account accession notes | A keyed reading that pairs an account-named feature with related image detail, including an explicit “not shown” state where the illustration offers no view | Call visual labels descriptive, not canonical names; do not assign an unlabelled image detail to an account-named object or treat a schematic as technical evidence |
| Atlas-view phase keys | A compact comparison of a visitor-facing depicted phase and the related written account, with each layer labeled and spatial or event claims kept within its source | Describe the Atlas as a view; do not treat the drawing or motion as independent proof of a route, mechanism, or in-world schedule |
| Cutaway object boundary cards | A structured inventory of a proposed room illustration that maps pictured objects to cautious readings and explicitly unrecorded names, uses, and ownership | Treat the cutaway as an editorial proposal; don't infer an established collection, practice, or production process from proximity |
| Process-to-use handoff cards | A concise cross-folio bridge between a recorded material-making process and its separately described use | Keep the adjacent stages distinct; leave transport, quantity, timing, and loading procedure open unless a source records them |
| Shelf-content boundary cards | A compact key that describes shelves and reading spaces shown in a proposed room plate without assigning titles, collections, or catalog order | Do not claim that a proposed record is depicted, held, or indexed unless the image or an account identifies it |
| Recollection concordance tables | A side-by-side reading of an established event account and proposed personal recollections, tracking shared sequence context and conflicting sensory detail | Keep each voice interpretive unless identified by a source; don't treat narrative overlap as witness corroboration or resolve disagreements by inventing a fact |
| Cross-plate concordance tables | A feature-by-feature comparison of two proposed views, noting shared motifs and one-view details | Treat repetition as illustration continuity, not independent confirmation; leave scale, bearing, material, and real-world use unverified |
| Cross-subject motif boundary cards | A comparison of similar visual symbols appearing in different place illustrations, recording each motif's local role and whether it is a plan mark, depicted object, or established account term | Similar appearance does not establish shared meaning, authorship, bearing, or a common instrument; keep each image's source status visible |

## Hourly substantial work cycle

On each run:

1. Review this file and the current Encyclopedia pages for recent additions, duplicate ideas, and the next useful open item.
2. Brainstorm at least one candidate content type or refinement and record worthwhile ideas in the table above.
3. Choose one open backlog item and deliver a substantial, coherent contribution with meaningful reference value, such as a well-developed folio section or new folio. A brief paragraph, minor wording change, or backlog-only edit does not qualify. If evidence or scope blocks substantial treatment, choose another item rather than pad content or invent canon. Mark completion with the page path and date.
   - Give every new folio its own subject-specific main image (Plate I), with descriptive alt text and a caption. Use a generated image when it materially improves the entry and fits the site's visual language; do not reuse an unrelated folio image as its main plate.
   - For a new place, institution, practice, or catalogued object, make the source boundary auditable: pair established references with proposed details and explicit unknowns, and link the new folio back to at least one relevant established folio.
   - Keep every Encyclopedia entry reachable from its index and searchable under its established name and useful topic terms; verify both after a new folio is added.
4. Add at least one new, specific backlog item that grows a different part of the setting; do not add duplicates or claim deferred simulation features exist.
5. Update `library/changelog.md` with a concise dated entry linking to the changed folio. Commit the cycle's files and push to `origin/main`, staging only files changed for this cycle; the site's GitHub Pages workflow publishes pushes. Do not send email or messages.
