# Vesperglass Encyclopedia: content ideas and backlog

Working tracker for new reference forms and encyclopedia additions. Keep it in step with the site and update it during each scheduled encyclopedia-growth run.

## Editorial guardrails

- Treat the atlas and currently written setting as the source for established names and facts.
- Label reconstructions, proposed details, and invented examples as **interpretation**; do not silently promote them to canon.
- Separate setting lore from implemented simulation behavior. Expeditions, gathering, refining, and persistent operations remain planned unless the site itself changes.
- Keep folios readable as reference pages: clear subject, concise context, useful sections, image descriptions, and links to related entries.
- Prefer additions that connect places, materials, vessels, and journeys so the setting feels inhabited without making unsupported claims.

## Candidate content types

| Content type | What it can add | Editorial treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specimen cabinet cards | Distinct materials, observed traits, handling notes, and relationships to craft | Mark invented classifications and samples as interpretive until established elsewhere |
| Annotated keeper log pages | A dated observation or maintenance note that reveals routine and voice | Label as an imagined document; do not imply a real expedition system |
| Vessel and instrument dossiers | Parts, purpose, operating principles, and repair vocabulary for trains or observatory tools | Distinguish visible design from inferred function |
| Route and crossing records | Named Gate journeys, departure and arrival descriptions, and route terminology | Frame as setting history or interpretation; do not claim a live route simulation |
| Place customs and daily practices | How keepers use shared spaces, prepare for departures, or preserve records | Mark customs as proposals until adopted as established lore; present them as optional social practices, not required simulation steps |
| Stellar body field plates | A survey of a kind of remote world, its landscape, and possible materials | Use as illustrative worldbuilding, not as a claim about generated Galaxy data |
| Oral histories and remembered sayings | Conflicting recollections, local phrases, and knowledge passed between keepers | Attribute each voice to an imagined source and retain uncertainty |
| Glossary crosswalks | Short linked definitions connecting materials, machinery, places, and travel | Keep terms consistent with current page names and label proposed terms |
| Archive catalog cards | A single chart, instrument note, or record with provenance and a short annotation | Identify each card as a proposal unless the site establishes the record as canon |
| Materials handling labels | Small notes about how objects are packed, stored, or moved between places | Mark handling methods as interpretive unless directly established in a folio |
| World relationship indexes | A compact cross-reference showing established links among places, materials, vessels, and journeys | Show only documented relationships; do not imply a physical route or system where none is established |
| Keeper phrasebook slips | Short greetings or recurring expressions that make everyday interactions legible | Attribute phrases to imagined speakers or traditions and label them as interpretation until adopted |
| Seasonal light calendars | A compact record of how keepers might name recurring changes in daylight or starlight | Present as an imagined cultural reference, not a real-time cycle or simulation mechanic |
| Instrument care notes | Brief annotations about how an imagined keeper might protect or inspect a reconstructed instrument | Label the equipment and routine as interpretation; do not imply an implemented maintenance system |
| Catalog status ribbons | Compact provenance labels that tell readers what an archival example is, what it covers, and what remains unknown | Make the record's proposed or reconstructed status visible; never imply it came from Galaxy output |
| Relationship evidence tables | A cross-folio index that groups documented connections by lore, viewer navigation, or visual presentation | Cite the relevant folios and state the limit of each connection; do not turn editorial links into routes |

## Hourly substantial work cycle

On each run:

1. Review this file and the current Encyclopedia pages for recent additions, duplicate ideas, and the next useful open item.
2. Brainstorm at least one candidate content type or refinement and record worthwhile ideas in the table above.
3. Choose one open backlog item and deliver a substantial, coherent contribution with meaningful reference value, such as a well-developed folio section or new folio. A brief paragraph, minor wording change, or backlog-only edit does not qualify. If evidence or scope blocks substantial treatment, choose another item rather than pad content or invent canon. Mark completion with the page path and date.
4. Add at least one new, specific backlog item that grows a different part of the setting; do not add duplicates or claim deferred simulation features exist.
5. Update `library/changelog.md` with a concise dated entry linking to the changed folio. Commit the cycle's files and push to `origin/main`, staging only files changed for this cycle; the site's GitHub Pages workflow publishes pushes. Do not send email or messages.
