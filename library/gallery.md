## Illustrated record and source notes

This register brings together the illustrations and diagrams that accompany Vesperglass accounts: named-place views, Atlas panels, plans, and figure studies. A card's **reading status** distinguishes an account-based view from an interpretation, an editorial drawing, or a picture whose source remains unassigned. Its **plate kind** describes the form of the image. These labels help a reader find a view and judge its limits; a proposed illustration does not establish a place's measured shape, construction, or furnishings. Search by subject or select several topic and plate-kind labels to narrow the register.

<h3 id="asset-kind-filters">Plate kinds · forms of illustration</h3>

Choose one plate kind to narrow the register, then combine it with topic labels. A kind distinguishes a place view from a plan, a field panel, or a sequence of poses; reading status separately records whether its details are interpreted, diagrammed, or source-unassigned. Neither label establishes the identity or history of a depicted object.

The register groups views as **Folio illustrations**, **Atlas place illustrations**, **Atlas background fields**, **Plans and diagrams**, **Figure studies**, or **Other illustrations**. The group appears on each card and can be found by name as well as by selecting a filter. Star Archive plates are set aside initially and can be included with the Archive control; the available kinds and topic labels adjust to the chosen set.

### Atlas destination views · eight named places

These eight Atlas views are paired with named destinations and linked to their corresponding accounts below. The descriptions name visible features so each place can be recognized in the register. The illustrations are interpretive views: their roofs, terraces, windows, gardens, and platform outlines are not measured records, and a resemblance to a folio plate does not confirm that the two pictures show one structure.

<h3 id="orbital-field-families">Clouded field studies · resemblance without lineage</h3>

Two nine-panel field studies show clouded reaches of blue and teal light. Each is arranged as a three-by-three set, with a row-and-column key distinguishing its panels. A representative panel from each study shows a different arrangement and density of luminous cloud and small points. These are descriptions of the pictured samples; the labels do not establish which study came first, whether one revises the other, or how the remaining panels meet at their edges.

The current Atlas presents a dark star field with a sparse layer of isolated lights behind its named-place views. The clouded studies are shown separately from that current background. This difference in visible role is useful for distinguishing the views, but it does not prove that either study once served as an earlier Atlas background. Nor does a row-and-column key make a panel a measured sky position: no coordinate key, celestial names, scale, or record of observation accompanies these studies.

| Reading question | Clouded field study A | Clouded field study B | Current Atlas background |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **What is visible?** | A representative square shows turbulent teal cloud, dark channels, and scattered small lights. | A representative square shows brighter cyan-blue cloud bands and more numerous scattered lights. | A dark field and a separate layer of sparse points frame the named-place views. |
| **What role is established?** | It is an image group in the register; no current Atlas use is identified. | It is a separately named image group; no current Atlas use is identified. | The dark field and sparse light overlay appear behind the current Atlas views. |
| **What remains unknown?** | Date, author, source, intended display, and relationship to the other clouded study. | Date, author, source, intended display, and whether the study is related to the first. | The identity of the lights, any correspondence to the clouded studies, and whether the background depicts a particular sky. |

<h4 id="clouded-field-panel-descriptions">Panel descriptions · position labels identify the image only</h4>

Each clouded field card now names its panel within its own three-by-three set and describes the visible contrast that distinguishes it: a brighter plume, a dark channel, a violet patch, or a scatter of small lights. **Upper left**, **center**, and similar labels refer to the panel's place in that arrangement. They are not a bearing, celestial coordinate, viewing direction, or match between the two separately unassigned studies.

| Card wording | Meaning within a pictured set | It does not mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Upper / middle / lower** | First, second, or third row in the displayed three-row arrangement. | Height above a horizon, latitude, declination, or an ordered observation. |
| **Left / middle / right** | First, second, or third panel across that row. | East, center of a sky, west, or a direction of travel. |
| **Center panel** | The cell in the second row and second column. | A central star, target, map point, or source for the surrounding panels. |
| **A / B field study** | An editorial distinction between two unassigned groups of clouded illustrations. | Earlier and later versions, related skies, or evidence that one study revises the other. |

The <a href="{{ '/library/star-archive/' | relative_url }}">Star Archive</a> is described as keeping knowledge of the sky, and the <a href="{{ '/library/observatory/' | relative_url }}">Observatory Logbook</a> as a place of observation and record. Neither account names the lights in these field studies or connects them with a chart or recorded observation. The <a href="{{ '/explore/' | relative_url }}">current Atlas view</a> and the named places it frames should therefore remain distinct from the unassigned clouded panels. The comparison records what the studies look like and how they are presently related to the Atlas; it leaves their provenance and subject open.

<h4 id="clouded-field-palette-comparison">Palette comparison · visible color, no physical reading</h4>

The two groups can be compared by the colors and contrasts visible in their panels. Study A often places deep blue around a teal plume, with violet accents appearing at some edges or lower reaches. Study B more frequently gives cyan and turquoise a bright central or crossing emphasis; blue remains prominent, while violet recurs beside some bright points and dark channels. These are broad readings of the illustrations, not measurements of every panel. The representative samples above are examples rather than a complete inventory.

<figure class="specimen-plate">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/library/atlas-clouded-field-palette-study.png' | relative_url }}" alt="An interpretive comparison of two separate cloudlike fields: the left emphasizes deep blue, teal, and violet, while the right has brighter cyan and turquoise amid blue and violet." loading="lazy">
  <figcaption><span>INTERPRETATION · PALETTE COMPARISON</span> This generated plate abstracts a broad color contrast between the two clouded field studies. It is not a reproduction, color sample, or measurement of either group; the paired panels do not establish a common subject, chronology, or source.</figcaption>
</figure>

| Reading point | Clouded field study A | Clouded field study B | Limit of the comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Most noticeable emphasis** | Deep blue surrounds or borders many teal-lit forms. | Cyan and turquoise often carry a brighter emphasis within the blue field. | “Emphasis” describes visual prominence, not greater energy or intensity in a measured sense. |
| **Secondary hues** | Violet appears in some clouds and along darker margins. | Violet appears beside blue forms and, in one panel, around a warm-looking bright point. | A hue in an illustration does not identify material, temperature, or a particular source of light. |
| **Dark areas** | Open dark channels interrupt several blue and teal forms. | Dark channels also separate some brighter cyan, blue, and violet shapes. | A similar contrast pattern does not establish the same physical structure or subject. |
| **Point-like lights** | Small points are comparatively sparse in the representative view. | Several panels show many small points among the clouds. | Their number or appearance does not identify stars, a distance, a field density, or a coordinate system. |

The safe conclusion is limited but useful: the pictures share a blue-and-teal family, while their visible emphasis differs, with A tending toward deep blue and B often showing brighter cyan or turquoise. The descriptions support an illustrated color comparison only. They cannot support a spectral reading, temperature, chemical composition, distance, celestial classification, or a claim that B is a later version of A.

This boundary matters because the <a href="{{ '/library/star-archive/' | relative_url }}">Star Archive</a> is established as a repository of sky knowledge and the <a href="{{ '/library/observatory/' | relative_url }}">Observatory Logbook</a> as a place of observation and record, but neither account names these clouds or links either group to an observation. The Atlas also uses a separate dark field behind named-place views; a shared blue palette alone does not connect those background lights with either clouded group.
