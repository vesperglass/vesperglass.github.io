## A service proposed from the architecture

This folio introduces the **Vesperglass Postal Service** as a proposed setting detail. The accounts consulted for this Encyclopedia do not record a postal office, a named postal keeper, or an established system for carrying correspondence. The seal and stamp designs below are new interpretive proposals, not transcribed artifacts.

The visual language draws on four places already named in the record: the freestanding ring of the [Vesperglass Gate]({{ '/library/vesperglass-gate/' | relative_url }}), the star-facing dome and telescope of the [Observatory Logbook]({{ '/library/observatory/' | relative_url }}), the glasshouse of the [Compass Garden]({{ '/library/compass-garden/' | relative_url }}), and the furnace-like roundel associated with [Tideglass Foundry]({{ '/library/tideglass-foundry/' | relative_url }}). These are proposed visual motifs, not evidence that those places once issued stamps or housed a mail room.

## Proposed first issue · four architectural motifs

The pictured sheet gathers four possible designs around landmarks already named in the Encyclopedia. Their titles are editorial labels for this proposed issue; no denomination, printing date, issue quantity, or order of release is established. The sheet is a fresh illustration for this folio, not a facsimile of a record.

The index records a subject for each panel, not four separate postal records. All four remain proposed designs arranged together for visual comparison. Their positions can be named without implying a first-to-last sequence, shared maker, local postal offices, or correspondence among the four places.

### Shared palette · color lineage across four source plates

The proposed sheet gives four different place motifs a common night-blue, teal, brass-gold, and parchment treatment. Comparing the sheet with its source plates shows both continuity and simplification: color helps hold the designs together as one graphic set, while each motif keeps its own local emphasis. These are readings of the illustrations, not confirmed material specifications for the places.

| Design and source | Color emphasis in the source plate | Treatment on the proposed sheet | What color does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Gate** · [Gate folio]({{ '/library/vesperglass-gate/' | relative_url }}) | Plate I depicts a teal-and-aged-brass ring against the stars. | The stamp retains a teal opening and warm gold bands, then adds a dark field and pale perforated edge. | The Gate's actual material, a postal color standard, or that its glass and metal have measured hues matching the artwork. |
| **Observatory** · [Observatory Logbook]({{ '/library/observatory/' | relative_url }}) | Plate I places a brass-colored telescope beneath a glass dome open to the stars. | The stamp pairs the dome and telescope with the same night-blue field, etched-looking gold, and parchment border used across the sheet. | A uniform finish for the Observatory, a recorded instrument color, or an actual view made at a particular hour. |
| **Compass Garden** · [Garden folio]({{ '/library/compass-garden/' | relative_url }}) | Plate I shows curved teal panes, pale star-shaped blooms, and luminous fern-like forms. | The stamp keeps the teal glasshouse and lifts one pale flower into a larger badge-like emblem. | A plant's natural color, named species, a shared botanical palette, or a Garden-issued stamp. |

The common treatment belongs to the proposed stamp suite: dark star fields, gold linework, and pale perforated borders create visual unity, while the four images retain different source subjects and accents. The [Gate comparison](#gate-stamp--motif-source-record) and [Foundry comparison](#foundry-stamp--motif-and-source-comparison) explain two of those motif transfers; the [Garden trace](#compass-garden-stamp--floral-motif-trace) follows the isolated flower. Taken together, they document design choices only. The surviving place descriptions and plates do not set a common architectural palette, confirm the illustrated materials, or record a postal printer or stock.

The strongest link remains the architectural motif of dome and telescope. The [Observatory instrument inventory]({{ '/library/observatory/' | relative_url }}) likewise labels pictured instruments cautiously and records no particular observing session.

### Observatory stamp · decorative stars and chart margin marks

The four large gold stars in the proposed Observatory stamp frame its miniature architecture. The proposed margin key for *The Unfixed Meridian* uses three different signs—**↻**, **?**, and **□**—to qualify written notes. Their marks can be compared because both are small symbols associated with sky material, but they perform different editorial jobs: one composes an image; the other labels the status of a proposed annotation.

| Comparison point | Observatory stamp · upper-right panel | Proposed *Unfixed Meridian* margin | What the pairing establishes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Visible marks** | Four prominent gold star forms sit near the panel corners, amid finer decorative scatter. | A return arrow, question mark, and open square precede sample phrases in a proposed margin. | The forms and positions can be described in their respective illustrations and examples. |
| **Role in the composition** | Corner stars help frame the dome, telescope, crescent, and field as a compact stamp image. | The marks qualify neighboring phrases as revisited, uncertain, or unlocated. | The stamp's marks are decorative framing; the chart key's marks are proposed reading-status labels. |
| **Relation to a sky subject** | No individual star, grouping, coordinate, or target is identified. | The key says it does not locate a body; even the sample's “pale band” remains unnamed and unplaced. | Neither design supplies a usable star position or identifies the same sky feature. |
| **Record status** | The Postal Service and its stamp sheet are interpretive design proposals. | The chart, title, notation, and sample margin are proposed; none is confirmed by a surviving catalog. | The comparison is between two editorial proposals, not a historical stamp and an authenticated chart. |

The distinction matters for indexing. A reader may use “corner stars” to locate the stamp's graphic framing, but should not treat those shapes as the chart's **↻** return mark or as a constellation key. Conversely, the chart's question mark is attached to uncertain wording, not to a star in the stamp. Neither side confirms the other's origin, notation, or source.

The [Star Archive margin legend]({{ '/library/star-archive/' | relative_url }}) defines each proposed mark within its own sample. This comparison leaves their relationship to the [Observatory Plate I sky]({{ '/library/observatory/' | relative_url }}) open as well: no date, orientation, target, author, or shared chart is supplied by the matching subject of stars.

### Foundry stamp · motif and source comparison

The Foundry stamp needs a more careful source label than its issue caption suggests. Plate I depicts a luminous circular feature framed by brass-colored machinery; Plate II shows a mirrored mill, a sifting tray, and a closed vessel reconstructed from the preparation account. The postal design turns a bright round opening into a furnace-like emblem. That is a coherent graphic choice, but neither Foundry plate identifies the round feature in the stamp as a particular piece of architecture.

| Question | Proposed Foundry stamp | Tideglass Foundry Plate I | Tideglass Foundry Plate II and account | Editorial boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **What is the central form?** | A round, bright center sits within a heavy brass-colored frame; the stamp reads it as a furnace motif. | The plate shows a working interior with a large mirrored mill, vessels, and processing equipment. | Plate II reconstructs a mill, sifting tray, and tempering vessel; the account names cleaning, grinding between mirrored plates, sifting, and tempering. | The stamp's round emblem is not an identified Foundry window or furnace in either plate or the process account. |
| **What carries the image?** | A dark blue field, engraved-looking gold frame, stars, and perforated parchment edge create the stamp's postal style. | The interior is a full workshop scene, with daylight at the windows and machinery across the room. | The tool plate isolates reconstructed equipment against a plain paper ground. | The miniature frame and night-sky field are design additions, not recorded Foundry finishes or postal stock. |
| **Which process does it name?** | The image suggests heat or a glowing aperture. | The plate depicts starshard preparation as a workshop scene, but does not label a specific pictured glow as a process stage. | The account says dust is tempered in the Gate's low resonance until its scattered glints settle into one quiet color. | The stamp does not document a furnace, temperature, heating step, or an alternative to the recorded resonance. |
| **What is the strongest supported connection?** | Its caption associates the round emblem with Tideglass Foundry. | The Foundry is the named place where starshards are prepared. | The written sequence connects the Foundry's work to stardust used by the train. | The place association is editorial; the stamp is not evidence of a workshop seal, maker, production batch, or postal issue. |

The distinction is useful because the stamp's warm central glow can easily be read as a literal process illustration. The Foundry account, however, describes a sequence and the settled appearance of the material, not a furnace architecture or heat source. The [Foundry folio]({{ '/library/tideglass-foundry/' | relative_url }}) keeps the reconstructed tools beside the operations they may illustrate; [Starshards & the Gate Train]({{ '/library/starshards/' | relative_url }}) follows the material into the train without filling in a missing Foundry mechanism.

**Source status:** the Foundry name and preparation sequence are established in their folio. The workshop and tool images are illustrative reconstructions. The stamp is a proposed design whose furnace-like reading belongs to the postal artwork; no account establishes that feature as a real Foundry window, furnace, or mark.

### Compass Garden stamp · floral motif trace

The lower-left design on the proposed four-stamp sheet pairs a glasshouse with one enlarged, pale, star-shaped flower held above its roofline. Comparing the two images makes the stamp's borrowing legible while keeping the small emblem from becoming a named plant or a specimen record.

The strongest supported link is therefore a borrowing between illustrations: a proposed stamp turns one element of the Garden plate into a compact emblem. ### Reading the overprint

The proposed mark is an image layered across a stamp design. Its circular form can create the familiar impression of a cancellation, but no account establishes that Vesperglass uses cancellations, what ink or tool would make one, or whether a mark would be applied once or more than once. The register therefore treats the linework as a design study only.

No cancellation motif converts a visual reference to one of those places into evidence of mail travel.

## Proposed seal · the open ring

The separate circular seal study repeats the Gate's ring and sets a small star above it. Its open center leaves the field visible rather than enclosing a destination. This is a graphic reading of the Gate image, not an official device preserved in an account. No ink, wax, embossing method, custodian, or authorization practice is recorded.

## What a stamp would and would not record

A stamp can mark a piece of correspondence within the proposal, while its architectural image names a point of reference. It cannot, by itself, establish where a message began or ended. The records provide no postal route, dispatch schedule, recipient list, fee, delivery interval, or proof-of-receipt convention. The Gate's documented role as the train's passage between worlds is not a record of mail being carried through it.
