## A service proposed from the architecture

This folio introduces the **Vesperglass Postal Service** as a proposed setting detail. The accounts consulted for this Encyclopedia do not record a postal office, a named postal keeper, or an established system for carrying correspondence. The seal and stamp designs below are new interpretive proposals, not transcribed artifacts.

The visual language draws on four places already named in the record: the freestanding ring of the [Vesperglass Gate]({{ '/library/vesperglass-gate/' | relative_url }}), the star-facing dome and telescope of the [Observatory Logbook]({{ '/library/observatory/' | relative_url }}), the glasshouse of the [Compass Garden]({{ '/library/compass-garden/' | relative_url }}), and the furnace-like roundel associated with [Tideglass Foundry]({{ '/library/tideglass-foundry/' | relative_url }}). These are proposed visual motifs, not evidence that those places once issued stamps or housed a mail room.

The motif-to-source relationship is editorial rather than postal: the Gate, [Observatory Logbook]({{ '/library/observatory/' | relative_url }}), [Compass Garden]({{ '/library/compass-garden/' | relative_url }}), and [Tideglass Foundry]({{ '/library/tideglass-foundry/' | relative_url }}) are the subjects named by the designs. A stamp picture cannot show that correspondence was carried to, from, or between those places.

The single-stamp study adapts features of the Gate illustration into a compact postal image. This record traces visible correspondences and identifies what the design changes; it does not authenticate a stamp, a postal service, or the Gate's construction.

| **Parchment border** | No postal frame appears in the Gate account or plate. | Perforations and a double-line frame identify the image as a proposed stamp design. | Postal dimensions, printing method, issue date, denomination, or actual use. |

#### Source boundary

| Layer | What is supported | What remains unestablished |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Named place and passage** | The Vesperglass Gate is a separate platform and the train's passage between worlds. | An office, stamp issue, postal keeper, dispatch, or mail route. |

The stamp's clearest lineage is therefore graphic: ring, palette, and a simplified crown echo the Gate plate, while its lower base and postal frame belong to the proposal. The [Gate folio]({{ '/library/vesperglass-gate/' | relative_url }}) keeps the pictured architecture distinct from the bloom described in the crossing account; [Words of Passage]({{ '/library/words-of-passage/' | relative_url }}) distinguishes the Gate passage from a place or route. Neither connection turns this stamp study into a record of correspondence.

### Foundry stamp · motif and source comparison

The Foundry stamp needs a more careful source label than its issue caption suggests. Plate I depicts a luminous circular feature framed by brass-colored machinery; Plate II shows a mirrored mill, a sifting tray, and a closed vessel reconstructed from the preparation account. The postal design turns a bright round opening into a furnace-like emblem. That is a coherent graphic choice, but neither Foundry plate identifies the round feature in the stamp as a particular piece of architecture.

| Question | Proposed Foundry stamp | Tideglass Foundry Plate I | Tideglass Foundry Plate II and account | Editorial boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **What is the central form?** | A round, bright center sits within a heavy brass-colored frame; the stamp reads it as a furnace motif. | The plate shows a working interior with a large mirrored mill, vessels, and processing equipment. | Plate II reconstructs a mill, sifting tray, and tempering vessel; the account names cleaning, grinding between mirrored plates, sifting, and tempering. | The stamp's round emblem is not an identified Foundry window or furnace in either plate or the process account. |
| **What carries the image?** | A dark blue field, engraved-looking gold frame, stars, and perforated parchment edge create the stamp's postal style. | The interior is a full workshop scene, with daylight at the windows and machinery across the room. | The tool plate isolates reconstructed equipment against a plain paper ground. | The miniature frame and night-sky field are design additions, not recorded Foundry finishes or postal stock. |
| **Which process does it name?** | The image suggests heat or a glowing aperture. | The plate depicts starshard preparation as a workshop scene, but does not label a specific pictured glow as a process stage. | The account says dust is tempered in the Gate's low resonance until its scattered glints settle into one quiet color. | The stamp does not document a furnace, temperature, heating step, or an alternative to the recorded resonance. |
| **What is the strongest supported connection?** | Its caption associates the round emblem with Tideglass Foundry. | The Foundry is the named place where starshards are prepared. | The written sequence connects the Foundry's work to stardust used by the train. | The place association is editorial; the stamp is not evidence of a workshop seal, maker, production batch, or postal issue. |

The distinction is useful because the stamp's warm central glow can easily be read as a literal process illustration. The Foundry account, however, describes a sequence and the settled appearance of the material, not a furnace architecture or heat source. The [Foundry folio]({{ '/library/tideglass-foundry/' | relative_url }}) keeps the reconstructed tools beside the operations they may illustrate; [Starshards & the Gate Train]({{ '/library/starshards/' | relative_url }}) follows the material into the train without filling in a missing Foundry mechanism.

**Source status:** the Foundry name and preparation sequence are established in their folio. The workshop and tool images are illustrative reconstructions. The stamp is a proposed design whose furnace-like reading belongs to the postal artwork; no account establishes that feature as a real Foundry window, furnace, or mark.

### Compass Garden stamp · floral motif trace

The lower-left design on the proposed four-stamp sheet pairs a glasshouse with one enlarged, pale, star-shaped flower held above its roofline. The [Compass Garden Plate I key]({{ '/library/compass-garden/' | relative_url }}) uses *starflower* only as a descriptive label for clusters of small, pale, radial blooms shown among the beds. Comparing the two images makes the stamp's borrowing legible while keeping the small emblem from becoming a named plant or a specimen record.

| Feature | Compass Garden · Plate I | Proposed stamp · lower-left design | Boundary of the comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Flower form** | Several small pale star-shaped blooms appear in clusters near the beds and pictured keeper. | One pale, stylized flower is enlarged above the conservatory roof. | The resemblance supports a visual reference, not a species identification, petal count, or claim that one flower was copied from an observed specimen. |
| **Setting** | The blooms sit within a glasshouse scene that also includes fern-like fronds, beds, a keeper figure, and the floor's compass rose. | The stamp reduces the scene to a frontal glasshouse beneath the single floral emblem. | The stamp omits the pictured keeper, planting beds, ferns, and compass rose; it does not document the Garden's actual arrangement. |
| **Graphic treatment** | The plate presents flowers as part of a larger interior composition. | The stamp isolates the bloom as a centered, badge-like mark and places it over the roof. | This hierarchy belongs to the proposed postal artwork; no account assigns a heraldic, navigational, or official meaning to the flower. |
| **Name and provenance** | *Starflower* is explicitly a visual label; no plant catalogue or keeper's planting record is known. | The design is described as a flower motif for Compass Garden. | The flower has no established botanical name, maker, origin, issue date, or documented relationship to any postal authority. |

The strongest supported link is therefore a borrowing between illustrations: a proposed stamp turns one element of the Garden plate into a compact emblem. It does not show a flower taken from a bed, prove that keepers cultivate or recognize a species called “starflower,” or establish that the Garden issued or received mail. The stamp sheet and its postal framing remain proposals, as stated in the first-issue section above; the Garden folio separately leaves its pictured flora unnamed.

## Proposed cancellation-mark register · motifs without itinerary

A postmark-like overprint can be pictured as an ornamental countermark on the four-design sheet. The following register proposes three line motifs and the stamp images they might accompany. It does not transcribe a surviving cancellation or document a letter that passed through a postal office.

| Proposed mark | Visual reading | Could accompany | What the mark cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Open Gate ring** | Two incomplete circles crossed by a short star-ray, echoing the Gate's open center. | The Gate arch stamp, as a repeated architectural motif. | That a letter entered the Gate, crossed it, or was posted at the Gate. |
| **Window lattice** | Three fine vertical strokes crossed by a shallow arch, recalling a framed Workshop or Observatory window. | The Observatory dome stamp; the architectural resemblance is deliberately broad. | A counter, sender address, office, observer, or place of posting. |
| **Tempered arc** | A pair of offset curves around an unfilled center, suggesting a reflected light rather than a filled emblem. | The Foundry roundel stamp, as a visual echo of its proposed circular motif. | A processing stage, production batch, date, fee, or Foundry dispatch. |

### Reading the overprint

The proposed mark is an image layered across a stamp design. Its circular form can create the familiar impression of a cancellation, but no account establishes that Vesperglass uses cancellations, what ink or tool would make one, or whether a mark would be applied once or more than once. The register therefore treats the linework as a design study only.

| Evidence layer | What this folio can say | What remains open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Named architecture | The Gate, Observatory Logbook, Compass Garden, and Tideglass Foundry are named elsewhere in the Encyclopedia. | Whether any place houses a postal service or produces correspondence. |
| Visible motif | The four stamp images depict an arch, dome and telescope, glasshouse and flower, and furnace window. | Whether a keeper would recognize or select these motifs. |
| Proposed overprint | Three imagined marks can be paired with related images for a coherent visual issue. | Actual use, date, place, sender, addressee, route, destination, and delivery. |

This boundary matters because the [Train Dock]({{ '/library/train-dock/' | relative_url }}) and [Gate crossing account]({{ '/library/vesperglass-gate/' | relative_url }}) describe travel by the locomotive, while the [Words of Passage]({{ '/library/words-of-passage/' | relative_url }}) cross-reference distinguishes those travel terms from the atlas's guide path. No cancellation motif converts a visual reference to one of those places into evidence of mail travel.

## Proposed seal · the open ring

The separate circular seal study repeats the Gate's ring and sets a small star above it. Its open center leaves the field visible rather than enclosing a destination. This is a graphic reading of the Gate image, not an official device preserved in an account. No ink, wax, embossing method, custodian, or authorization practice is recorded.

## What a stamp would and would not record

A stamp can mark a piece of correspondence within the proposal, while its architectural image names a point of reference. It cannot, by itself, establish where a message began or ended. The records provide no postal route, dispatch schedule, recipient list, fee, delivery interval, or proof-of-receipt convention. The Gate's documented role as the train's passage between worlds is not a record of mail being carried through it.

| Proposed element | Reference behind the image | Still unrecorded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gate-arch stamp and separate seal study | The Gate is named as the train's passage; its plate depicts a ring on a detached platform. | Whether a seal or stamp existed, and how either was applied. |
| Observatory stamp | The Observatory Logbook is a named place of observation and record. | Whether an office, telescope, session, or postmark was associated with it. |
| Compass Garden stamp | Compass Garden is named among the outer platforms and shown as a glasshouse in its folio plate. | Any postal connection, named flower species, stamp value, or issue date. |
| Foundry stamp | Starshards are cleaned, ground, sifted, and tempered into stardust at Tideglass Foundry. | A postal connection to the Foundry, stamp value, or issue date. |
| Postal service | No postal institution is established by the consulted accounts. | Its keepers, routes, schedules, rates, and delivery practices. |

The service remains a proposed addition until a later account establishes more.
