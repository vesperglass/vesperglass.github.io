## The place in the atlas

Compass Garden is a named glasshouse on one of Vesperglass's outer platforms. No surviving account lists its plants, says how they are maintained, or names its keepers.

## An architectural reading

The compass rose marks the garden's central point of orientation, with beds and curved paths arranged around it. No survey identifies a bearing toward a particular destination or records the full planting plan.

#### A cautious reading of the shared form

The Garden's repeated arches make its roof read as a long, transparent enclosure around beds and paths. The Observatory's more concentrated dome reads as a canopy over instruments directed toward the sky. This contrast is a useful way to read the pictures: one plate organizes attention around a glasshouse interior, the other around an observing room. It does not establish that glass is used for the same purpose in both places, that either structure opens or moves, or that the pictures record their actual architecture.

For the Garden's recorded place description and plan boundary, see the Plan key above. The [Observatory folio]({{ '/library/observatory/' | relative_url }}) distinguishes its established record-keeping role from the instruments and room arrangement pictured in its plates. Neither source connects the two structures beyond this visual comparison.

## A proposed light calendar · three garden readings

No surviving account gives Compass Garden a calendar, names a season, or describes a cycle of changing light. The following three markers are an interpretive vocabulary for a keeper's notes, composed for this folio. They do not establish dates, duration, a source star, or a recurring celestial pattern.

| Proposed marker | Light cue a keeper might record | Possible note beside the garden plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First Reach** | A narrow line of star-facing light touches one of the outer marks around the compass rose. | Draw the edge of the light on a blank plan and leave its direction unassigned. |
| **Open Span** | The line extends across the rose, making its center and the nearest planting beds visible together. | Compare the shadow at the center with the earlier mark; note any difference without assigning a cause. |
| **Quiet Edge** | The line no longer crosses the center mark, though the glasshouse remains lit. | Keep the previous mark for comparison and leave space for another observer to disagree. |

These are reading prompts, not a sequence of guaranteed seasons. A keeper might encounter them in another order, fail to observe one, or choose different names. The proposed entries describe where light appears in relation to the garden's compass rose; they do not claim that its plants respond on a schedule.
