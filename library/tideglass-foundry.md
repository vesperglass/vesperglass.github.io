## The place in the atlas

Tideglass Foundry is the Vesperglass platform where starshards are prepared. The recorded account says they are cleaned, ground between mirrored plates and sifted into a fine powder. The dust is then tempered in the Gate’s low resonance to become stardust for the train’s starwell.

## An architectural reading

The room and instruments on these plates expand that process into an architectural scene. The mill is small and deliberate: the work depends on reading a shard’s internal planes and preserving a clean, even powder. The source account names the mirrored plates and sifting, but does not record the room's dimensions or the mill's maker.

## Process-to-plate key · interpretive assignments

The account gives a sequence of work; Plate II gives a reconstructed set of tools. This key places them side by side so that a pictured instrument is not mistaken for a tool named in the account. The order in the first column follows the account; the proposed matches in the third column are readings of the illustration.

| Stage in the account | What the account records | What Plate II depicts | Boundary of the match |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cleaning | Starshards are cleaned of their host stone before grinding. | No cleaning instrument is identified on Plate II. | The account names the operation but supplies no tool, technique, or measure of completion. |
| Grinding | Shards are ground between mirrored plates. | A small hand-cranked mirrored mill. | Mirrored plates are named; the mill, crank, dimensions, and arrangement are reconstructed. |
| Sifting | The ground material is sifted until it is a fine, even powder. | A glass sifting tray. | Sifting is recorded; the tray's material, mesh, and form come from the plate, not a specified account. |
| Tempering | The dust is exposed to the Gate's low, singing resonance until scattered glints settle into one quiet color. | A brass-framed vessel shown closed. | Resonance and the settling appearance are recorded. The vessel, its closure, and any device that carries the sound are not specified. |

The correspondence is therefore uneven by design: cleaning has no pictured counterpart, while the later operations have possible visual counterparts. The [Starshards & the Gate Train folio]({{ '/library/starshards/' | relative_url }}) describes the material's signs and the finished stardust; [Words of Passage]({{ '/library/words-of-passage/' | relative_url }}) distinguishes that material from the sealed starwell that carries it. Neither supplies missing Foundry equipment details.

This is an editorial key, not a recipe or a record of an observed work shift. The accounts provide no quantities, grinding pressure or speed, sieve opening, duration, temperature, resonance instrument, or measured yield. The plate cannot fill those fields.

### Process-to-use handoff · tempered dust and the starwell pulse

The Foundry account follows starshards through cleaning, grinding, sifting, and tempering. A separate passage in [Starshards & the Gate Train]({{ '/library/starshards/' | relative_url }}) describes how the finished stardust serves the locomotive. Read together, the accounts connect the material made at Tideglass Foundry with the train's fuel role; they do not describe a particular batch being transferred or loaded.

| Account stage | Recorded description | Supported connection | Still unrecorded |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tempering at Tideglass Foundry** | The dust meets the Gate's low, singing resonance until its scattered glints settle into one quiet color. | This is the final named Foundry operation; the prepared powder is called stardust. | The source of the resonance, its duration or strength, and any measurable color standard. |
| **Stardust as train fuel** | The locomotive feeds a measured thread of stardust into its sealed glass heart; the grains gather into a single pulse. | The train account assigns the prepared material a use in the starwell. | The amount in a measured thread, feed rate, chamber dimensions, or the pulse's duration. |
| **Preparation-to-use link** | The Foundry account says stardust is prepared for the train's starwell, and the train account describes stardust within that chamber. | The named material and its broad purpose connect the two accounts. | Whether both descriptions concern one batch, how it reaches the train, who carries it, or when loading takes place. |

The two descriptions mark different stages. **Quiet color** names the settled appearance after tempering; **pulse** names the response described when grains gather in the starwell. The accounts do not say that the color causes the pulse, that the Gate's low resonance is the mechanism inside the chamber, or that tempering continues during a crossing. Those would join separate descriptions with a process the records do not give.

The handoff therefore ends at a useful boundary: the Foundry makes stardust for the train, and the locomotive uses a measured thread in its starwell. No transfer vessel, loading action, quantity, schedule, responsible keeper, or specific crossing is recorded. [Words of Passage]({{ '/library/words-of-passage/' | relative_url }}) defines *stardust* and *starwell* without adding those missing steps.

### Terminology note · “mirrored plates” and “quiet color”

The recorded process uses concise, sensory wording for both a named tool surface and the appearance after tempering. This note reads those phrases against the [Starshards & the Gate Train account]({{ '/library/starshards/' | relative_url }}), where the full sequence is stated, and the [Words of Passage crosswalk]({{ '/library/words-of-passage/' | relative_url }}), which distinguishes stardust from the train's starwell and passage terms. These are readings of account language, not additional production instructions.

| Account phrase | What the account says | Safe reading | What the wording does not specify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **“mirrored plates”** | Starshards are ground between mirrored plates. | A plural pair of surfaces is named as the grinding context. | Their material, polish, shape, size, mounting, pressure, motion, maker, or whether “mirrored” refers to reflectivity in a technical sense. |
| **“fine, even powder”** | Sifting continues until this is the described powder state. | The phrase gives a qualitative result for the sifted material. | Grain-size range, distribution, sieve mesh, mass, yield, or a numeric completion test. |
| **“low, singing resonance”** | The dust is exposed to the Gate's resonance during tempering. | “Low” and “singing” describe the account's character of the resonance. | Frequency, loudness, source, instrument, distance, duration, or a measured acoustic condition. |
| **“scattered glints”** | Before the described settling, the dust's glints are scattered. | The account describes an appearance associated with the material before the final color phrase. | A count of glints, particle behavior, emitted light, energy, or a physical explanation for the change. |
| **“one quiet color”** | The scattered glints settle into this described result. | “Quiet” qualifies the visual phrase; it is not itself a color value or a claim that sound stops. | Hue, brightness, calibration, persistence, causal mechanism, or a link to the later starwell pulse. |

Read together, the terms move from a named grinding relation, through a qualitative powder and resonance description, to a visual result. The language supports a verbal account of sequence and appearance. It does not provide the measurements or equipment details needed to reproduce the process, and the reconstructed mill and vessel on Plate II cannot supply those omissions.

The distinction also matters when the material reaches the train. The Foundry account ends with stardust whose glints settle into “one quiet color”; the separate train account describes a measured thread gathering into a pulse inside the starwell. A shared material links the passages, but the color phrase does not explain, cause, or predict the pulse. No transfer event or continuous tempering process connects the two descriptions.
