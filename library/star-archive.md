## The place in the atlas

## An architectural reading

The circular arrangement allows a reader to move from the shelves to a central table without losing sight of the room’s shape. A skylight and chart cabinet connect the archive to the sky, while distinguishing it from the Observatory’s work of looking outward. The exact plan is not recorded.

### Title word and map-feature boundary · “Meridian”

*Meridian* is part of the proposed title written on this page. It is not a transcribed label from an Archive catalog, and no surviving account establishes that a chart with this title exists. The card's proposed scope is only a teaching sheet for comparing broad bands of light across an otherwise unmarked sky. The title alone does not tell us whether “Meridian” is a technical term, a metaphor, or simply a name chosen for the editorial example.

Read the title as a name whose meaning is unresolved. A familiar technical-sounding word cannot supply missing evidence about the chart's contents: a named meridian would require some source for its placement or use, and neither is present here. Likewise, *unfixed* does not tell us what might be fixed, by whom, or against which reference. Those details remain unassigned rather than silently filled in from the word's ordinary associations.

This distinction also keeps neighboring sections in scope. The proposed **□ · place unverified** mark records that a place-name lacks a confirmed position; it does not stand for a meridian or coordinate system. ### Proposed margin-mark legend · *The Unfixed Meridian*

The chart card above suggests leaving room for later annotations. This key proposes three marks for that margin, each describing the status of a note rather than locating a body in the sky. The surviving accounts do not supply these symbols; they are editorial proposals for the named chart, whose existence and title also remain unconfirmed.

| Proposed mark | Reading | Limit of the mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **↻ · returned to** | A reader has set down a second look beside an earlier note. | It does not establish that the same observer, instrument, or conditions were used. |
| **? · uncertain reading** | The writer cannot decide between two readings of a feature. | It does not resolve the feature or identify its cause. |
| **□ · place unverified** | A place-name has been retained without a confirmed position. | It does not mark coordinates, a route, or a confirmed destination. |

#### Sample margin · proposed

> **↻** pale band noted again; comparison not dated · **?** edge may belong to the band or an adjacent mark · **□** place-name retained; position unverified

This sample is composed to demonstrate the key, not transcribed from a chart or an observer's notebook. It assigns no new name or destination. The marks preserve a distinction among recurrence, uncertainty, and missing location; they do not turn the three notes into a sighting record.

Use the return mark only to point back to earlier wording, not to claim a repeated event. Keep a question mark attached to the unresolved phrase, not to a presumed object. Use the open square to flag a name whose place remains unknown, never as a map pin. This keeps the chart's possible annotations legible without assigning a sky position or travel route.

The [Observatory sample leaves]({{ '/library/observatory/' | relative_url }}) also leave the subject and position open, but their prose comparison is separate from this proposed chart notation. Neither record identifies a point on the Galaxy map.

### Phrase concordance · “pale band” and “blue-field point”

The proposed margin sample says **“pale band noted again”** beside the return mark. The Observatory's invented sample describes **“one point”** near the rim of a blue field and says its color held through three turns of a sighting tube. Both texts invite a second look at a light-colored feature, but their wording points to different forms and comes from separate editorial examples. This concordance records the echo without joining the notes into one observation.

| Reading dimension | *The Unfixed Meridian* · proposed margin | Observatory Logbook · proposed sample leaves | What the comparison can and cannot say |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Feature described** | A “pale band” is mentioned, with no shape, extent, or location beyond the sample phrase. | A “point” sits near the rim of an otherwise unnamed blue field. | A band and a point are not interchangeable descriptions; neither wording names a star or other body. |
| **Return language** | **↻** accompanies “noted again,” referring to an earlier note within the imagined chart sample. | The first leaf records three turns; the proposed second leaf revisits the point after the tube returns to a mark. | Both signal recurrence in their own prose, but there is no shared return protocol or confirmed repeated event. |
| **Color** | “Pale” modifies the band, without a prior color reading or comparison. | The first leaf says the point held its color; the second proposes that it seemed paler. | The color terms are not measurements and do not show that the two phrases refer to one changing feature. |

| **Position and time** | No date, coordinate, orientation, observer, or fixed sky region accompanies the margin sample. | “Near the rim” is a relative phrase; the leaves have no date or fixed coordinates. | Their unlocated positions cannot be aligned with each other or a point on the Galaxy map. |

The phrase **“noted again”** should be read narrowly: it signals that the proposed margin writer is referring back to earlier wording. It does not establish how much time passed, whether another observer saw the same thing, or whether the feature persisted. The Observatory sample is similarly open: its return to a mark is part of an imagined account, not evidence of a calibrated instrument or a confirmed celestial change.
