## The light that can be carried

Among the bright seams found on remote stellar bodies, there is sometimes a crystal that seems to hold a little of the sky within it. The travelers of Vesperglass call these fragments **starshards**. They are gathered intact and brought home in padded, dark-glass cases; a shard is not a lamp, and it gives no useful light until it is worked.

The oldest Foundry account describes a starshard as “a piece of night remembering the shape of day.” Its blue-black faces catch the color of the star that formed it. When turned, a thin gold line appears beneath the surface: a trace that the shard can be coaxed to release.

<div class="shard-signs">

### Cabinet records · interpretive specimens

These proposed cabinet notes group three forms by their visible traits. The surviving accounts do not establish a formal scientific classification.

<dl class="folio-glossary">
  <div><dt>Blue seam</dt><dd>A compact shard with one clear inner line. A useful teaching specimen for showing how a bright seam can hint at the star beneath which a crystal formed.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Split heart</dt><dd>A larger fragment whose memory planes divide around a central fracture. Foundry hands might sort it for careful grinding rather than treat the split as a defect.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Quiet glass</dt><dd>A dark, low-glimmer shard whose light appears only when turned. Its cabinet note would record the angle and conditions of observation before assigning any meaning to its color.</dd></div>
</dl>

#### Reading the label

The first two fields summarize the account’s stated material and transit. They establish that the shard is carried intact in a padded, dark-glass case; they do not tell us how the case is opened, whether it is sealed, how it is oriented, or what happens at receipt. The remaining fields are proposed blanks for a cataloguer to preserve those absences. They are not evidence that a particular case arrived, that a receiver inspected it, or that the Foundry uses this form.

This distinction matters because the three cabinet forms above—*Blue seam*, *Split heart*, and *Quiet glass*—are proposed interpretive notes, not a classification recorded by the Foundry. A label can refer to one only when its proposed status stays attached. The case description by itself does not establish that any of those examples was carried, received, or examined.

For the wider material account, see [Tideglass Foundry]({{ '/library/tideglass-foundry/' | relative_url }}), where the recorded sequence is cleaning, grinding between mirrored plates, sifting, and tempering. That process begins after the starshard reaches Vesperglass; neither folio supplies a procedure for opening or moving its transit case.

### Field plate · the Blue-Sill Basin

The Blue-Sill Basin field account describes a shallow depression where a dark ridge crosses a pale mineral shelf. No surviving survey identifies its coordinates or confirms a starshard deposit there. The place name and terrain description are preserved as an interpretive account.

Fine blue seams catch the low light along the ridge's sheltered side. The account notes scattered glints but gives no confirmed deposit, coordinates, or survey result.

## How a shard becomes stardust

Raw starshards travel first to the **Tideglass Foundry** at Vesperglass. There, the shards are cleaned of their host stone, ground between mirrored plates, and sifted until only a fine, even powder remains. The last step is called *tempering*: the dust is exposed to the Gate's low, singing resonance until its scattered glints settle into one quiet color.

The finished material is **stardust**. In a sealed vessel it looks almost still. Tip the vessel and a slow aurora passes through it, as if each grain were trying to remember a direction.

### Material state card · sealed and tipped

This proposed comparison card keeps the account's two descriptions beside one another. It is a reading aid for the language of the folio, not a test result or a vessel-handling instruction.

| Reading in the account | Described appearance | What the description does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vessel sealed | The stardust looks almost still. | Its quantity, packing, exact vessel construction, or whether “still” describes anything beyond its appearance. |
| Vessel tipped | A slow aurora passes through the dust. | How far or quickly the vessel moves, how long the light lasts, or what causes the effect. |

The contrast is a change in what the observer sees: near-stillness in the sealed vessel, then a slow aurora when it is tipped. “Aurora” is the account's image for the passing light, not a measured motion or explanation of its cause. No material, dimensions, closure design, or method of handling the vessel is recorded here. The [Tideglass Foundry folio]({{ '/library/tideglass-foundry/' | relative_url }}) describes how stardust is made; the [Words of Passage]({{ '/library/words-of-passage/' | relative_url }}) cross-reference distinguishes the material from the starwell that carries it.

## A furnace that does not burn

The train's fuel chamber is called the **starwell**. It does not burn stardust as an ordinary furnace burns coal. Instead, the locomotive feeds a measured thread of dust into its glass heart. The grains answer one another, gathering into a single pulse that lets the train lean against distance and find the Gate's far side.

That is why the train is more than a vehicle. It is a moving instrument tuned to the Gate: brass for its frame, dark glass for its heart, and stardust for the note that opens a road. When a train leaves at speed, the portal gathers around it in a bright, expanding bloom. On arrival the bloom opens outward again, and the locomotive emerges already carrying the light of its return journey.

### Starwell · account and image boundary

The account names the train's fuel chamber the **starwell** and describes it as sealed glass. It says the locomotive feeds a measured thread of stardust into its glass heart, where the grains gather into a pulse for crossing. These are account details about a role and an effect, not a mechanical cross-section.

Plate I shows the locomotive from outside as it passes through the Gate's bloom. A bright circular feature is visible at the front of the engine, but the image does not expose a chamber interior, show stardust, or label that feature as the starwell. Figure I arranges the material sequence as a reference diagram; its small train symbol is schematic and adds no construction detail.

| Question | Account | Plate I / Figure I | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **What is named?** | A sealed glass fuel chamber called the starwell. | The train exterior and a schematic train symbol are pictured. | No image label identifies a specific visible part as the starwell. |
| **What does it carry?** | Stardust is carried for a Gate crossing. | The exterior image shows the train in transit, amid the Gate bloom and scattered star-like fragments. | The image does not establish where the material sits or whether any pictured fragments are inside the locomotive. |
| **What happens to the material?** | A measured thread is fed into the glass heart; the grains gather into a pulse. | No loading action or internal movement is shown. | The account provides no chamber dimensions, feed mechanism, quantity, timing, or technical diagram. |
| **What is the chamber made from?** | The starwell is described as sealed glass; the train's heart is described as dark glass. | Plate I is a luminous exterior scene rather than a material sample or cutaway. | The exact glass, wall structure, closure, and relationship between visible light and chamber material remain unspecified. |

The shared phrase **glass heart** connects the account's chamber with the locomotive's fuel role, but it should not be used to infer that the circular light at the engine's front is an exposed or transparent starwell. The plate offers no interior view against which to check that reading. The [Train Dock folio]({{ '/library/train-dock/' | relative_url }}) describes the locomotive's berth and departure, while [Words of Passage]({{ '/library/words-of-passage/' | relative_url }}) defines *starwell* as the sealed chamber; neither supplies the missing construction view.

### Inner pulse and outward bloom · two described scales

The crossing account moves from stardust gathering in the starwell to the Gate's bright bloom around the train. These descriptions belong to the same broad account of passage, but they name different things at different scales: a response attributed to grains in a sealed chamber, and light that surrounds and briefly conceals the locomotive as a road takes shape. Their neighboring place in the prose does not establish that one is the visible form of the other.

| Comparison point | Starwell · inside the locomotive | Gate · around the locomotive | What remains open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Where is the event placed?** | In the train's sealed glass fuel chamber, its glass heart. | Around the train as the Gate passage takes shape. | No cross-section or view identifies an interior part with the surrounding bloom. |
| **What is described?** | A measured thread of stardust feeds into the heart; grains gather into a pulse that lets the train lean against distance and find the far side. | A bright, expanding bloom gathers around the train, briefly hides it, and opens outward at emergence. | The account does not say that the pulse looks like rings, emits the bloom, or is seen outside the train. |
| **How are they related?** | The pulse is assigned a crossing role in the train account. | The bloom marks the road taking shape and the train's emergence. | Their shared crossing context is stated; a direct causal link, exact order, duration, or synchronization is not. |
| **What can the plates verify?** | Plate I does not show the starwell interior or feeding of dust. | Plate I presents the train amid luminous Gate imagery, not a technical account of the event. | Neither plate supplies a mechanism, energy path, or diagram joining chamber and Gate. |

The [Vesperglass Gate folio]({{ '/library/vesperglass-gate/' | relative_url }}) records the bloom in its own passage terms: it gathers around the locomotive, conceals it briefly, and opens as the train appears on the far side. Read alongside the starwell account, that is a useful comparison of inner response and outer passage imagery, not evidence that the two lights are one process. The account leaves unanswered whether the pulse precedes the bloom, whether the Gate answers it, or whether “pulse” is visible at all.

**Comparison boundary:** “pulse” belongs to the description of stardust gathering in the starwell; “bloom” belongs to the description of light around the train. This folio keeps their distinct locations and stated roles visible and does not supply the missing connection.

### Container roles · case, sealed vessel, and starwell

Several vessels appear along the starshard-to-stardust account, but their similar glass descriptions do not make them one object. The transit case carries raw starshards home; a later passage describes finished stardust at rest in a sealed vessel; and the train's fuel chamber is separately named the **starwell**. Keeping these records side by side makes the material sequence readable without supplying a missing container handoff.

| Account reference | Contents and stated role | Construction or state actually described | Place in the sequence | Relationship left open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Transit case** | Holds starshards intact while they are brought home. | Padded, dark glass; no closure or interior arrangement is described. | Before Foundry preparation. | Whether it is opened at the Foundry, reused, or carries anything after arrival. |
| **Sealed vessel** | Contains finished stardust, which looks almost still when sealed and shows a slow aurora when tipped. | Sealed; its material and design are not specified in that passage. | After the powder is made; the observation describes its appearance. | Whether this is a storage vessel, a sample vessel, or the train's starwell. |
| **Starwell** | The locomotive's fuel chamber carries stardust; a measured thread feeds into its glass heart and gathers into a travel pulse. | Described as sealed glass. The chamber interior is not shown in Plate I. | In the train's crossing account. | Whether its glass is dark, what part receives the measured thread, or which vessel holds the dust beforehand. |

#### What the accounts connect

The material path is supported at a broad level: starshards arrive intact, are prepared at the [Tideglass Foundry]({{ '/library/tideglass-foundry/' | relative_url }}), and become stardust used by the train's starwell. The account names the transformation and the fuel role. It does not identify the containers as a continuous set of vessels, say that a transit case becomes a storage vessel, or describe pouring, packing, loading, or receipt.

| Secure connection | Source detail | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shard → Foundry work** | Raw starshards are brought home intact; the Foundry account records cleaning, grinding, sifting, and tempering. | No receiving moment or transfer from case to work surface is recorded. |
| **Foundry work → stardust** | The prepared powder is named stardust after tempering. | No quantity, yield, packaging, or interval between stages is given. |
| **Stardust → train crossing** | The train uses a measured thread of stardust in its starwell to form a pulse. | No loading scene, feeder design, timing, or link to the sealed-vessel observation is specified. |

The distinction is especially important for the dark-glass case and the starwell: both mention glass, but only the starwell is called the train's fuel chamber, and only the case is described as padded and used to carry intact starshards home. The [Train Dock folio]({{ '/library/train-dock/' | relative_url }}) records the locomotive's berth and departure, not a materials-loading procedure; [Words of Passage]({{ '/library/words-of-passage/' | relative_url }}) defines the name *starwell* without adding a second vessel to its account.

**Record boundary:** the three names above track distinct descriptions in the accounts. Treating the sealed vessel and starwell as the same object, or assigning one case to both raw shards and finished dust, would go beyond what is recorded.

<aside class="field-note"><span class="field-note-mark" aria-hidden="true">✳</span><p><strong>A note from the keepers</strong><br>The bloom is a passage taking shape, not an explosion. The train is never consumed by the light; it is briefly hidden by the road it has made.</p></aside>
