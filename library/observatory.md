## The place in the atlas

Observatory Logbook is the named observatory among Vesperglass's outer platforms. The name joins a place of looking with a record of what is seen, but surviving accounts preserve no particular observation, archive catalogue, or resident astronomer.

## An architectural reading

The room places instruments and written notes side by side. The orrery offers a way to compare positions, while the logbook keeps observations in a form that can be revisited. Its architectural details and exact measurements are not preserved in the accounts consulted here.

## A leaf from the logbook

This interpretive entry separates a recorded observation from a possible explanation and a follow-up check. Its provenance is unverified, so it should not be used as a dated measurement of a named star.

### Comparison slip · a second watch

Set beside the first leaf, this proposed follow-up asks whether the point looked the same when the sighting tube returned to its mark. No date, observer name, or field coordinate accompanies the comparison.

<aside class="field-note"><span class="field-note-mark" aria-hidden="true">✳</span><p><strong>Proposed second leaf · no date recorded</strong><br><em>At the mark left beside the blue-field point, its color seemed paler when the tube returned to that position. The rim of the field moved as I turned the eyepiece; I cannot tell whether the point changed or the sighting changed. Leave both notes together for another reader.</em></p></aside>

<dl class="folio-glossary">

  <div><dt>What differs</dt><dd>The first says the color held through three turns; the proposed second leaf reports a paler appearance after the tube returned to its mark.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>What remains unknown</dt><dd>Without a date, fixed coordinates, or an independent comparison, the pair cannot establish whether the light changed, the instrument shifted, or the viewing conditions differed.</dd></div>

These two leaves are an editorial comparison, not authenticated Observatory entries. Their value is in showing how agreement, difference, and uncertainty can sit together without turning an open question into a conclusion. No particular star or event is identified.

The note records what the observer thinks they saw, what should be checked next, and which explanation remains uncertain. Its language and procedure are proposals, not established Observatory practice.

### Status ribbon · reading the sample leaves

The comparison is easier to use when its several kinds of certainty stay visible. This proposed ribbon is an editorial key for the two sample leaves; it is not a mark copied from an Observatory register.

| Status | Application to this folio | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PLACE · named** | Observatory Logbook is a named destination among Vesperglass's outer platforms. | The exact room arrangement, its keeper, or a particular observing session. |
| **LEAVES · proposed** | The blue-field note and its second-watch comparison are examples written for this folio. | Authenticated entries, dates, authors, or a preserved logbook. |
| **OBJECT · unnamed** | Both leaves describe a point near a blue field's rim. | A named star, planet, or other body. |
| **POSITION · unassigned** | The wording gives a relative position within the imagined view. | Coordinates or a fixed mark that another reader can identify. |
| **GALAXY MAP · unmatched** | No point in the separate Galaxy map is identified by either leaf. | That a displayed point corresponds to the described light or that a map feature was observed at the Observatory. |

Read together, the ribbon separates a real place-name from a proposed document and from the absent identity of the thing described. The first two leaves can be compared as writing: one says the color held through three turns, while the follow-up proposes a paler appearance and notes that the sighting changed. Without a date, coordinates, or an identified object, neither leaf can confirm a change in the sky. The status key leaves that question open instead of turning the map, the prose, or their resemblance into proof.

The proposed light readings in [Compass Garden]({{ '/library/compass-garden/' | relative_url }}) use a similar discipline: they locate a described cue in relation to a garden marker but do not supply a date or celestial cause. Each record keeps its own subject and uncertainty; neither one names an Observatory target.

## Proposed instrument care note · the star lens

The Observatory plates show a brass telescope, but the surviving accounts do not name a particular lens, describe its mounting, or record how an instrument is maintained. The following care note is an editorial reconstruction for the pictured star lens. It is not a keeper's instruction, a record of actual work, or evidence of an Observatory maintenance system.

### Reconstructed care card

<aside class="field-note"><span class="field-note-mark" aria-hidden="true">✳</span><p><strong>Proposed card · star lens · no instrument record identified</strong><br><strong>Before observation:</strong> In this proposed scene, a dust cover is lifted from the lens and set aside. Its material, fit, and storage place are unspecified.<br><strong>Cloth:</strong> A clean cloth is imagined beside the instrument and used for the cover's exterior only; the proposal does not depict it touching the glass. No account records this use.<br><strong>After observation:</strong> The cover may be returned in an illustrative sequence. No keeper, date, inspection, or maintenance event is recorded.<br><strong>Record status:</strong> proposed equipment and practice, reconstructed from the Observatory imagery.</p></aside>

The card deliberately separates a possible accessory from an actual act. A dust cover makes the lens legible as an instrument with a period of rest between observations; the cloth's proposed use is restricted to the cover so the imagined scene does not turn into unsupported glass-cleaning advice. Neither gives us grounds to claim that keepers polish the glass, use a particular material, or follow a routine.

The comparison with the logbook is useful: the proposed observation leaves explicitly preserve what the observer could not determine, while this care card preserves what the instrument account does not say. Both are editorial aids, not a technical manual or evidence of a recorded Observatory practice. The [Star Archive's proposed *Unfixed Meridian* catalog card]({{ '/library/star-archive/' | relative_url }}) offers a separate example of keeping an instrument-linked record distinct from a confirmed object or observation.
