## Terms used in accounts and atlas views

Several terms describe different parts of a train's passage. Some belong to the accounts of travel; one names a path suggested by the train's movement at its berth. This cross-reference gathers them without making the terms into an operating sequence or adding a route between the Gate and the Train Dock.

<dl class="folio-glossary">
  <div><dt>Stardust · account</dt><dd><p>Fine powder made by cleaning, grinding, sifting, and tempering starshards at Tideglass Foundry. Accounts describe it as the material carried for a crossing.</p><p><strong>Still open:</strong> No measure, vessel capacity, or quantity for a particular journey is recorded.</p></dd></div>
  <div><dt>Starwell · account</dt><dd><p>The locomotive's sealed glass chamber. Stardust gathers there into the pulse that lets the train lean against distance and find the Gate's far side.</p><p><strong>Still open:</strong> The chamber's dimensions and engineering are not specified.</p></dd></div>
  <div><dt>Gate bloom · account</dt><dd><p>The bright light that gathers around the train as the Gate passage takes shape, briefly hiding the locomotive before the bloom opens outward again at arrival.</p><p><strong>Still open:</strong> The accounts assign no schedule, duration, or destination to a particular crossing.</p></dd></div>
  <div><dt>Guide path · atlas view</dt><dd><p>At the Train Dock, the atlas shows the locomotive following an unseen alignment as it approaches, berths alongside the platform, and departs. “Guide path” names that reading of the displayed movement.</p><p><strong>Still open:</strong> The path is not drawn as rails, and it does not establish a fixed route between the separate Dock and Gate platforms.</p></dd></div>
</dl>

### Where the terms meet

The accounts connect stardust to the starwell and the starwell to passage through the Gate. The Gate bloom describes the light around that crossing. The guide path belongs to the separate view of the train aligning with its berth at the Dock. These descriptions can be read together, but they do not make the Dock's alignment a track to the Gate or establish the details of any particular journey.

For the material and its place in the locomotive, see [Starshards & the Gate Train]({{ '/library/starshards/' | relative_url }}) and [Tideglass Foundry]({{ '/library/tideglass-foundry/' | relative_url }}). For the light and the berth, see [The Vesperglass Gate]({{ '/library/vesperglass-gate/' | relative_url }}) and [Vesperglass Train Dock]({{ '/library/train-dock/' | relative_url }}).

### Place words · platform, berth, and Gate

These words describe different kinds of relation in the travel accounts. A platform is a named place in the archipelago; a berth is where the locomotive rests alongside the Train Dock; the Vesperglass Gate names both a separate platform and the passage the train makes through it. Keeping those uses distinct prevents a shared vocabulary from implying one continuous structure or a mapped route.

| Term | Use in the Encyclopedia | Keep distinct from | What the term does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Platform** | A general description for the floating places of Vesperglass, including the Workshop, Threshold House, Train Dock, and Gate platform. | A single building type or an architectural specification. | That every platform has the same dimensions, foundation, arrangement, or function. |
| **Vesperglass Train Dock** | A named platform and the locomotive's place to berth alongside its open edge. | The train's berth position and the separate Gate platform. | A rail station with tracks, an enclosed terminal, or a route to the Gate. |
| **Berth / alongside** | A position beside the Train Dock's edge, leaving the platform clear. | A track, coupling device, or dock-to-Gate path. | Exact offset, orientation, attachment, or a measured stopping point. |
| **The Vesperglass Gate** | The name of the separate platform and, in crossing accounts, the passage between worlds. | The Train Dock and its guide path. | A door-like mechanism, a continuous structure joining both platforms, or a fixed geographic distance between them. |

The wording moves between a place and its role: **the Gate** can mean the named destination in the archipelago or the passage described in a crossing account. The context identifies which use is intended. In both cases the Train Dock remains separate, and its guide path belongs to berth alignment; no surviving account supplies a fixed route between the two platforms. See [the Gate folio]({{ '/library/vesperglass-gate/' | relative_url }}), [the Train Dock folio]({{ '/library/train-dock/' | relative_url }}), and [Threshold House]({{ '/library/threshold-house/' | relative_url }}) for the distinct place entries.

## Reader pronunciation key · English-language cues

No surviving account records how Vesperglass travelers pronounce these terms. The cues below are a practical guide for reading the printed names aloud in English; capital letters show the suggested emphasis, not a local or canonical pronunciation.

| Term | Suggested syllables | Reader cue | Use in the folios |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Vesperglass** | ves · per · glass | **VES**-per-glass | The place-name Vesperglass. |
| **Tideglass** | tide · glass | **TIDE**-glass | The name in Tideglass Foundry. |
| **starwell** | star · well | **STAR**-well | The locomotive's sealed chamber. |

The word breaks follow familiar English parts: *Vesper* with *glass*, *tide* with *glass*, and *star* with *well*. Keeping the first part audible helps distinguish the place-name from the Foundry name and the train chamber, even when the terms appear close together in a sentence. The suggested stress gives a steady reading across the glossary; it does not settle how speakers in Vesperglass sound, or how readers of other English dialects should shape each vowel.

For a short practice, say the three labels in sequence—**VES**-per-glass, **TIDE**-glass, **STAR**-well—then return to the definitions above. The spellings and referents are recorded in the folios; the syllable breaks and emphasis here remain an editorial aid.
