## Connections, with their limits

This index brings together relationships already described in the Encyclopedia. It separates **setting lore** from **atlas navigation** and **visual presentation** so that a reader can follow an idea across pages without mistaking a reference link for a physical route or a working simulation system.

| Connection | What the pages establish | Kind of relationship | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starshards → Tideglass Foundry | The lore describes starshards being brought to Vesperglass and prepared at the Foundry. | Setting lore | The browser viewer does not implement gathering, transport, or refining. |
| Tideglass Foundry → stardust → train starwell | Foundry work turns shards into stardust, and the train's starwell carries that material for a crossing. | Setting lore | No active crafting, inventory, or fuel-consumption system is implied. |
| Train Dock ↔ Vesperglass Gate | The Dock and Gate are separate atlas platforms; the lore names the Gate as the train's passage between worlds. | Setting lore + atlas presentation | Their separation does not establish a track, fixed route, or persistent train operation. |
| Train Dock → free flight | The atlas animation shows the train approach, berth alongside the Dock, depart, and return to free flight. | Implemented visual presentation | The loop is not a scheduled trip, a recorded arrival, or a persistent departure event. |
| Vesperglass Gate → Galaxy view | Selecting the Gate in the viewer opens the procedural Galaxy view. | Implemented atlas navigation | Opening that view does not simulate the train crossing or establish a particular destination as canon. |
| Vesperglass Workshop → the wider atlas | The Workshop is described as Vesperglass's central home platform and a place from which the other platforms are understood. | Setting description + atlas organization | “Central” describes its role in the setting and atlas; it does not by itself establish walkable paths or an operating expedition loop. |

### How to read the distinctions

**Setting lore** records relationships described in the fictional account. **Atlas navigation** describes what a viewer selection opens. **Visual presentation** names an animation the site displays. These can refer to the same subject, but they are different kinds of evidence: the train's passage through the Gate belongs to lore, while selecting the Gate to open the Galaxy is viewer navigation.

The Encyclopedia also links related folios for reading. Those links are editorial cross-references, not additional world geography. The claims above are limited to what the referenced pages currently say; interpretive plates, imagined customs, and proposed records do not add new routes or implemented behavior.
